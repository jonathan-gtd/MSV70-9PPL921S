Anyone ever retrofitted a sport button on the E90?

It came on Z4s and some E60s. On those cars it also affected the suspension & steering, most E90s don't have active steering though.

It would be relatively trivial. the switch is around $45, and you need 3 pins - 5v for the LED (I'd probably use 12v and a ~400ohm resistor), ground, and signal from the DME.

The Z4 switch could be hidden in the cigarette lighter:

part # 61316953942. The connector is # 61136909058. BMW only sells the pins with a pigtail (for like $3 a piece), I already have a bunch of them I got from a generic source.

it looks like it alters the torque targets and pedal inputs. I believe this map here is for throttle control, either directly or indirectly by influencing torque scaling:



There's a simple on/off switch for enabling the sport switch. That might satiate the people who think that the stock pedal mapping isn't very responsive. You could even do it without a switch - just ground the appropriate DME pin and leave it in "sport" mode all the time. Not sure what it feels like but I kinda want to try it. a $1 rocker switch from a hardware store would be a good temporary solution to see if it's worth doing.

Obviously, it doesn't increase power. Might it still might make it more enjoyable to drive - especially downshifts. I find rev matching difficult sometimes.

The E60 setup is different. The Z4 switch connects straight to the dme.

Quote:
Dynamism Control (FDC)
The Dynamism Control (FDC) function is enabled using the Sport button. With the FDC function enabled, the DME engine management system switches to a more sports-oriented characteristic map.

Effect for the driver:

When the Sport button is actuated, the engine reacts more quickly to the accelerator pedal module. This means that less pedal travel is required to accelerate. The function enables the driver to adopt a more sports-oriented driving style.

The signal of the Sport button is forwarded by the DME engine management system to the Electronic Gearbox Control (EGS or SMG).
