why do you need to know the lift at idle? That's sort of irrelevant to how the VVL mechanism works.

What you really need to know (if you are going to modify a rocker ratio or something similar) is the relationship between eccentric cam position and valve lift with the cam set to peak opening. There is a table that models just that, and is what the DME uses to determine how far to rotate the eccentric shaft to reach a commanded valve opening.

WOT is also irrelevant. Your foot position doesn't correlate 1:1 with the valve lift. Even at recognized full load, valve lift isn't a fixed value - it's a curve that varies depending on RPM. Then add factors such as demanded torque, perceived demand, etc. which all play a factor.

we do know the eccentric cam position. It's right here:


This is the map you would have to alter. This is basically the shape of the rocker ratio - you would need to plot it out again from whatever you modified and enter it back into this table. The VVL control is a PID loop and it will go very bad if this curve doesn't match the physical response of the eccentric shaft & valve lift.

I don't know how you could alter only the end of the eccentric shaft/rocker travel without affecting the beginning. Notice how it doesn't increase lift much with eccentric shaft movement at the beginning, and then it starts to increase faster until it's fully open - this is purposeful and important to low-speed and low-load engine control.

I doubt the N55 is identical but it would have similar maps. Not every N52 has the same valvetronic settings either. The 328i and 325i generally have less lift in every map.

You aren't understanding what I'm trying to tell you. The DME doesn't respond to load by increasing the valve lift - it does it the other way around. It looks at what the driver is asking for (requested torque, basically) and adjust valve lift to a given torque output. The load follows - it already knows what the VE is going to be from valve lift.

The system works because it always knows what the valve lift is, and it has a model of airflow for a given valve lift through different engine states. You can't rely on the O2 feedback to paper that over.

You're trying to just force it to open the valves further without the DME knowing (and you can't just change the valve lift tables, because those are targets, not actual values). It's going to act like a vacuum leak, only worse - because the PID loop is going to expect a certain airflow (and torque) for a given eccentric shaft position, only now you've messed with the slope of the curve and the response isn't going to be what it expects. If you're lucky the PID loop won't go crazy and oscillate to hell but I wouldn't hold my breath.

but what do I know? I've only been staring at the disassembly for months.

Again, it's only a target. If you just modify the rocker ratio, and change the "target" to match, but don't change the the eccentric shaft table - it's can't rotate the eccentric shaft to the point you're telling it to, because for example it thinks that 180 degrees is 10.1mm of lift, but you're telling it to go to 10.2mm of lift, which in reality is 172 degrees due to the modifications. It isn't going to rotate the eccentric shaft to 181 degrees where your target would be on that curve, because it's not phsyically possible.

The result is it's going to think it's at 9.7mm (~172 degrees) because it doesn't really care if the target is higher than what is possible (you can set the target to 11mm or 12mm or whatever - it won't change at all past ~9.9mm). Now there's going to be this .5mm difference between the expected valve lift and the actual valve lift, which if you believe is a 5-10% increase in airflow, is going to result in a 5-10% error between the model and reality, not to mention the unknown effects on a PID loop that isn't responding linearly.

You even have it in your post - that it looks at the expected EC position for a given valve lift (not really, it actually looks at the actual valve lift which is derived from the EC position). That is the part you are breaking.
